# Business case

Reviewed **24 October 2026**. Civic helps drivers in England understand a parking notice and prepare a more complete, evidence-backed challenge. The core user is the driver/notice recipient; the council officer workflow is a separate retained pilot capability.

## The need

A notice can leave someone uncertain about the issuer, process, deadlines, supporting evidence and what to write. These steps are spread across the notice, issuer portal and guidance. Civic brings the preparation work into a single case, while keeping the driver responsible for checking facts and submitting it.

The homepage uses dated evidence of the scale of the problem: **5.17m London parking PCNs in 2024/25**, and **16.9m private-parking keeper lookups in 2025/26** as broader UK context. The latter is an enforcement proxy, not unique people or a complete count of tickets. POPLA cancellations and one borough's enforcement revenue add context but do not measure Civic's opportunity or success directly. [Exact definitions, methods and sources](CONSUMER.md#homepage-figures).

The team's informal finding that 60% of people spoken to liked the idea is a discovery signal. Without sample size, recruitment method and recorded responses it is not a representative demand estimate or a conversion forecast.

## Value proposition

| User need | Implemented response |
|---|---|
| Understand the notice | Photo extraction/manual entry, explicit confirmation and issuer/stage distinction |
| Make a clear case | Evidence checklist, source-linked assessment, risks and editable draft |
| Complete the next step | Approved appeal pack, external submission guidance, calendar download and response tracking |
| Find parking next time | Public London map, recorded restrictions, filters and directions |
| Try before registering | Fixed sample ticket with live Mubit and labelled fallback |

The differentiator to test is a coherent workflow from notice to evidence pack, with traceable guidance and user control. No unsupported claim of superiority to named competitors, guaranteed savings or automatic legal representation is made.

## Business model and validation

Public signup is implemented; charging, subscriptions and paid tiers are not. Per-case or subscription pricing remains a hypothesis. Measure completion, willingness to pay, support cost, AI cost/case and retention before choosing a commercial model. Map use alone is not evidence of appeal revenue.

A pilot should record preparation time, factual corrections, independently reviewed draft quality and confirmed outcomes, with consent. Distinguish notices cancelled before payment from actual refunds. Do not extrapolate a selected appeal success rate to every ticket or treat user reports as verified savings. [Metrics](05-metrics.md).

The council workflow could support a separate B2B pilot, but requires approved policies, procurement/privacy work and authorised supplier integration. It is not a universal council connector or an implemented subscription business.
